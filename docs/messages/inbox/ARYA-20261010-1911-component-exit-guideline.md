Michael explicitly described this as Deal Sniffer's absolute objective: "I want to look at these things and piece them out." The assessment should reflect his practical mechanical and welding capability, rather than treating a nonrunning asset as automatically worthless or unsuitable.

Required assessment:
- Consider mutually exclusive exit paths: sell whole as-is; repair and resell; part out; repurpose or convert. Explain which path fits the available evidence and why. Do not double-count the whole asset's sale value plus the same components' sale values.
- Itemize recoverable components such as engine, trailer, controls and other usable parts. Separate visible condition, seller claims, tested facts and unknowns. Inspect actual listing photos; obtain missing evidence rather than infer serviceability or capacity.
- Use relevant sold comparables for each proposed exit/component where available. Keep asking prices apart from realized sales, account for condition/compatibility and realistic demand, and label unsupported resale estimates unknown or provisional.
- A nonrunning whole asset may still be attractive if supported component recovery justifies the total cost and effort. Do not automatically reject it solely because it does not run.
- Include actual needed parts, work, fuel, fees, paperwork/title, disposal, storage and time to sell. Account for realistic liquidation, unsold components and residual disposal rather than assuming everything sells promptly at the best asking price.
- Respect Michael's mechanical and welding skill. Do not automatically price all labor or transport as hired services; distinguish work he can do, work requiring outside help, time/opportunity cost and actual cash outlay. Do not assume tools, transport capacity or unlimited available time.
- Repurposing needs a supported feasibility assessment. For example, a boat-trailer frame is not automatically suitable for a car trailer. Verify frame, axle, brakes, load distribution, manufacturer GVWR/GAWR and applicable engineering/safety requirements; unknown capacity stays unknown. Skill does not replace structural or capacity evidence.
